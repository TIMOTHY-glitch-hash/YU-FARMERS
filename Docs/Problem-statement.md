# Problem Statement

## Background

Agriculture is an important source of food, employment and income for many households in Uganda. However, many smallholder farmers continue to face difficulties accessing reliable and profitable markets for their agricultural produce. Farmers commonly depend on middlemen, informal traders, personal networks and local markets to sell their products. These channels often provide limited access to potential buyers and market information, while prices may vary significantly depending on location, season, demand and the bargaining power of the parties involved. Consequently, farmers may be forced to sell their produce at low prices, especially when they need to sell perishable products quickly or when there is a high supply in the market.

## Buyer-Side Challenges

Buyers such as restaurants, retailers, wholesalers, institutions and agro-processors often experience difficulties identifying farmers who can reliably provide the required type, quantity, quality and location of agricultural produce. Finding suitable suppliers through informal networks can be time-consuming and may involve multiple intermediaries. Buyers may also have limited ability to verify the availability, quality and reliability of a supplier before making an agreement. This can lead to increased transaction costs, delays, inconsistent supply and uncertainty regarding prices, delivery and payment.

## Gaps in Existing Solutions

Existing approaches to connecting farmers and buyers, including informal communication channels and some digital agricultural marketplaces, do not always address the wider challenges involved in agricultural transactions. Listing products on a platform alone does not necessarily establish trust, guarantee accurate information about available quantities, coordinate delivery or ensure that individual smallholder farmers can satisfy larger orders. Furthermore, challenges such as limited digital literacy, unreliable internet connectivity, seasonal production, fragmented supply, transportation constraints and uncertainty between buyers and sellers can reduce the effectiveness and adoption of digital market solutions.

## Resulting Market Gap

As a result, there is a gap between agricultural supply and market demand. Farmers may fail to reach suitable buyers or obtain favourable prices, while buyers may spend considerable time and resources searching for reliable suppliers. This contributes to inefficient market linkages, increased dependence on intermediaries and difficulties in coordinating the movement of agricultural produce from farmers to buyers.

## Proposed Solution

There is a need for an integrated digital platform that facilitates more organized and transparent interaction between farmers and buyers while addressing key challenges beyond simple product listing. The proposed system will provide a centralized marketplace through which farmers can register and create profiles, list available produce with relevant information such as quantity, price and location, while buyers can search for available produce and communicate their purchasing requirements. The system will also support mechanisms such as user verification, supplier matching, farmer aggregation for larger orders, ratings and reviews, transaction management, and coordination of transportation and delivery.

## Goals & Initial Scope

The proposed system aims to improve farmers' access to markets, reduce the time and effort required by buyers to identify suitable suppliers, increase transparency and trust between market participants, and improve the efficiency of agricultural transactions. The initial implementation will focus on a defined geographical area and selected agricultural products, allowing the system to be developed and evaluated under realistic local market conditions before considering expansion to other regions and agricultural value chains.
